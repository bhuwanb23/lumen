# 01 — Product brief

## One-sentence description
**Lumen helps people understand the wording in a cancer pathology or radiology report before an appointment, by turning report text into a cautious, source-linked briefing and questions for the care team.**

## User problem and context
Patients may see test results through portals or on paper before a clinician has explained them. Reports use clinician-facing language, abbreviations, and fragmented findings. Access does not guarantee comprehension; unfamiliar language can cause confusion and unwarranted worry. Printed/scanned reports are harder to navigate where portals are unavailable. The original brief cites studies about patient-centered report comprehension, immediate result viewing, and under-discussed trials. Any numerical claims in the pitch must be checked against primary sources before use; remove unverified numbers rather than guess.

## Intended users
- Adults preparing for a care-team appointment after receiving a cancer-related report.
- Caregivers helping with the patient's consent.
- People with limited medical vocabulary, limited English, or paper-only access.

Not intended for children, emergency care, clinical decision-making, or unsupervised treatment interpretation.

## Job to be done
> “Help me understand what this document says, show me where each explanation came from, and help me ask my care team useful questions—without telling me what my diagnosis or treatment should be.”

## Product promise
1. Explain only explicit statements in the supplied document.
2. Separate stated, unclear, and not-stated facts.
3. Provide short plain-language claims tied to exact source quotations.
4. Let a patient inspect each quotation.
5. Offer neutral questions for the care team.
6. Disclose uncertainty, missing/unclear text, language limits, and service failure.

## Principles
- Evidence before eloquence; an untraceable answer is a failure.
- “Not stated in this report” is better than a guess; absence is not a negative result.
- Scope safety is a feature: no diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment advice.
- Patient agency: prepare for a clinician conversation, do not replace it.
- Accessible by default and honest about supported languages.
- Privacy restraint: stateless handling and metadata-only logs.
- Distinguish planned, implemented, tested, and clinically validated.

## Success
A demo shows synthetic report → explicit facts with evidence → short claims that pass source and verifier checks → claim-to-source interaction → neutral questions → clear limits. It also shows reproducible evaluation and honestly states weaknesses. Success is not model fluency or feature count.

## Non-goals
No diagnosis/re-staging beyond the literal report; prognosis/survival/recurrence estimates; treatment or dosing recommendations; trial eligibility decisions; accounts, sharing, longitudinal tracking; clinical deployment or regulatory/compliance claims; replacing clinicians, interpreters, navigators, or emergency services.
